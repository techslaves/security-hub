What's New in the 2025 Top 10

Reflecting modern RAG architectures, autonomous agent deployments, and cloud financial exposure.

LLM10 • Expanded

Unbounded Consumption

Expands beyond traditional DoS to address GPU resource depletion and catastrophic cloud bills from "Denial of Wallet" (DoW) attacks.

LLM08 • New Focus

Vector & Embedding Weaknesses

Dedicated protection for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), vector databases, embedding inversion attacks, and cross-tenant context leaks.

LLM07 • New Risk

System Prompt Leakage

Counters extraction of confidential system preambles, embedded credentials, role boundaries, and internal business logic.

LLM06 • Expanded

Excessive Agency

Broadened for autonomous multi-agent systems, unvetted tool integrations, and permissions that enable destructive real-world actions.

Legal Obligations

NIS 2 Regulatory Foundations

The NIS 2 Directive establishes legal measures to boost the overall level of cybersecurity in the EU, imposing direct obligations on management and substantial non-compliance sanctions.

Entity Classification

Essential vs. Important

• Essential Entities (Annex I): Energy, transport, banking, healthcare, drinking/waste water, digital infrastructure (cloud, DNS, IXP, CDNs), B2B ICT (MSPs/MSSPs), public administration, and space.

• Important Entities (Annex II): Postal/courier, waste management, chemicals, food production, manufacturing, digital providers (search, marketplaces), and research.

Supervised under ex-ante (Essential) and ex-post (Important) supervisory regimes.
Fines & Personal Liability

Severe Statutory Sanctions

• Essential Entities: Fines up to €10,000,000 or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover (whichever is higher).

• Important Entities: Fines up to €7,000,000 or 1.4% of total worldwide annual turnover.

• Management Accountability: C-suite executives and board members can be held personally liable and temporarily banned from managerial functions.

Article 20 & 34 Directive (EU) 2022/2555.
Article 23 Notification

Mandatory Incident Reporting

Entities must notify their designated national CSIRT or competent authority for any significant incident:

• 24 Hours: Early warning indicating whether caused by unlawful/malicious acts.
• 72 Hours: Official incident notification with initial assessment.
• 1 Month: Final report including root cause, impact, and mitigations.

Applies to operational outages and significant security breaches.
